Swalpa Adjust Maadi

Dig up roads to cut off power and water from your opponents!

Players: 2-4

In the city of Bengaluru, footpaths and roads are constantly dug up to lay cables and pipes, and to build metro pillars and flyovers. The city's residents, left to deal with unpredictable power and water, tell each other: "Swalpa adjust maadi -- please bear with the inconvenience". This game is dedicated to the resilient people of this once-beautiful city.

Objective

Players are contractors laying power and water pipes on a board; one contractor's work often breaks another's pipes. Houses have storage tanks to deal with supply disruptions; each tank can store up to three units of power or water. To start, each player has four houses with empty tanks. A house with at least two units of power AND two units of water is SAFE. The first player to get to three SAFE houses wins.

Board

The board's top and bottom edges are the Power Supply, its left and right edges are the Water Supply. Each square is a tile. Park tiles are fixed. There are six types of Pipe tiles, each with a single power pipe and a single water pipe.

Pipe tiles

Houses

Houses owned by a player have roofs in that player's colour. Every house has a power tank (left) and a water tank (right) that tracks a house's power and water levels (0-3 tally marks). Score boxes below the board track the number of SAFE houses each player currently has.

A house can store power and water

Order of Play

Play happens in rounds with a rotating starting player: P1 starts Round 1, and other players follow; then P2 starts Round 2, and other players follow; etc. On their turn, each player makes their move by placing a Pipe tile on the board. Once all players have finished, the power and water levels of all the houses are tallied. Then the next round starts with a new starting player.

Marking Your Move

On your turn, select a square on the board and then one of the Pipe tile icons shown above the board. You can try up to five square/tile combinations. Your chosen square and tile are highlighted with an outline in your colour. Tap the chosen square or tile to confirm your move. The square you played on is then shaded grey. Off limits squares: Park squares, squares with houses belonging to other players, and squares previously played in the current round (shaded grey).

After: A new tile on the chosen square

In multi-player mode ("Human" icon) each player takes their turn in order. In Human vs. Bot mode ("Bot" icon) Player 1 is the human, other players are Bots, and each Bot's turn plays out automatically. On your turn you can use the Auto button to autoplay your move. Bot play and Autoplay are indicated by a PLAY icon in the score box.

Power and Water Supply

Each house is on a tile with a single power pipe and a single water pipe, and each pipe has two ends. To track power flow, start at one end of the house's power pipe and trace its path outwards. Only move along power pipes; stop if you hit a water pipe or Water Supply. If you hit a Park, move straight through without turning, and if you reach a power pipe on the other side, keep going. If this path ends at a Power Supply, then power flows to the house. (To track water flow, repeat the same steps for the house's water pipe.) Dark red shows paths with power flow, dark blue shows paths with water flow.

Before: Pipes with no supply
After: Power and water supply connected

Power and Water Tanks

Once all players have confirmed their move in a round, power and water flows to each house are tracked. If at least one end of a house's power/water pipe reaches a Power/Water Supply, its power/water level ticks up by one unit, up to a maximum of three. If neither end of a house's power/water pipe reaches a Power/Water Supply, its power/water level ticks down by one unit.

Before: Updated flows, current tanks
After: Updated tanks

Winning the Game

A house is SAFE if it has two units of power AND two units of water. The first player to hit three SAFE houses at the end of a round wins. If more than one player simultaneously reaches this target, the game is a draw.
