Swalpa Adjust Maadi

Dig up roads to cut off power and water from your opponents!

Players: 2-4

Components

A board
Tiles: PARK, PIPE
Tokens: HOUSE

Dedication

In the city of Bengaluru, footpaths and roads are constantly dug up to lay cables and pipes, metro pillars and flyovers. The city's residents, left to deal with unpredictable power and water, tell each other: "Swalpa adjust maadi -- please bear with the inconvenience". This game is dedicated to the resilient people of this once-beautiful city.

Objective

Players are contractors laying power and water pipes on a board. One contractor's work can break another's pipes, so houses store power and water to deal with supply disruptions. A house is knocked out once its power or water tanks go empty. Players survive as long as they have houses on the board, you must knock out all the opponents' houses to win. The residents of these houses must suffer in silence. Swalpa adjust maadi.

The Board

The board is a square grid (6x6 for 2 players, 7x7 for 3 and 4 players). It comes set up with a fixed set of PARK tiles, a set of PIPE tiles on all other squares, and three HOUSE tokens for each player. Score boxes track the number of houses each player has on the board, and how many units of POWER or WATER remain in each house's tank.

Power and Water Supplies

The board's top and bottom edges are the POWER SUPPLY, and its left and right edges are the WATER SUPPLY. The main goal of the game is to bring these resources INTO the board, to your own houses, by placing PIPE cards cleverly. The key friction in the game is that you cannot change a POWER PIPE without also changing a WATER PIPE on the same square.

Each PIPE tile has a single POWER PIPE and a single WATER PIPE crossing it. Each edge of a PIPE tile has a single pipe ending on it. When a PIPE tile is placed next to a SUPPLY edge, the resource will only flow into the square if both the SUPPLY and the PIPE are of the same type. If a POWER PIPE touches the WATER SUPPLY edge, the pipes are mismatched and water WILL NOT flow into the pipe.

If a POWER PIPE is connected to a POWER SUPPLY, this pipe can then supply power to another square, as long as it connects to a POWER PIPE on that square. Similarly, if a WATER PIPE is connected to a WATER SUPPLY, this pipe can then supply water to another square, as long as it connects to a WATER PIPE on that square. Power and water pipes can cross without blocking one another.

The Deck

There are 6 possible types of oriented PIPE tiles. Each player plays one tile per round.

The deck showing tile types

Playing a Round

At the start of each round, each player secretly chooses a PIPE tile (consisting of one POWER PIPE and one WATER PIPE) and a square on the board. All players then reveal their choices at once. Each player removes the POWER and WATER PIPES on their chosen square, and replaces them with pipes matching their chosen tile. Off-limits squares: No tile may be played on a PARK square. A player cannot play a tile on a square that has another player's HOUSE (unless that house has already been knocked out).

Collisions

If two or more players choose the same square, this is a collision. Any pipes presently on that square are struck out, so POWER and WATER no longer flow through it. These pipes stay broken until a player chooses that square on a subsequent turn.

A collision!

Knocking Out Houses

POWER and WATER will only flow into a house if the PIPES on its square match the PIPES on its edges, and those pipes are connected to a POWER/WATER SUPPLY. Each house has two tanks, one for POWER and one for WATER, each with a capacity of three units. At setup, all houses start connected to POWER SUPPLY and WATER SUPPLY, so both tanks are full with three units of each. At the end of every round, every house on the board is checked. If the house is still connected to POWER/WATER SUPPLY, then its corresponding tank FILLS UP completely. If it is not connected to POWER/WATER SUPPLY, then its corresponding tank FALLS by one unit. If either tank reaches zero, the house is knocked out before the next round begins.

Winning the Game

A player is eliminated the instant all their houses are knocked out. The last player standing wins. If every remaining player is eliminated in the same round, the game ends in a draw.

Playing on a Write & Wipe Sheet

Press the SAVE icon to export the present board to a WRITE & WIPE sheet. The same board is shown twice: left shows POWER SUPPLY and PIPES, right shows WATER SUPPLY and PIPES. The board sheet should be cut out and kept visible to all the players. This board sheet also lists score boxes for each player, showing the POWER and WATER tank levels on every house. These tanks start with three units each.

Each player also has to cut out a CHOOSE SQUARE card, on which each PIPE tile type is shown twice: top shows POWER, bottom shows WATER. On your turn, choose a specific POWER/WATER PIPE tile and a square on the board (using the coordinates A,B,... and 1,2,...). All players then reveal their CHOOSE SQUARE cards at once. If there is a collision involving two or more players, the pipes on the corresponding square are erased. For all remaining squares, players erase the present pipes and replace them with the pipes on their chosen POWER/WATER PIPE tile.

Once all players have played, every HOUSE on the board is checked. The POWER/WATER levels on each houses's tanks are updated based on whether the houses are connected to POWER/WATER SUPPLY (fill up completely if connected, fall by one unit if not connected). If either tank reaches zero, the house is struck out on the score box.

Playing on a Board

Set up the initial board using the template layouts provided (the 2-player 6x6 board has 4 PARK tiles and 32 PIPE tiles; the 3/4 player 7x7 board has 6 PARK tiles and 43 PIPE tiles). Use the MAIN/CROSS tokens to label the rows and columns on the board. Place the N/S cards on the board top and bottom to mark POWER SUPPLY, and E/W cards on the board right and left to mark WATER SUPPLY. Place each player's HOUSE tokens as indicated on the template layouts. Each HOUSE token has blank spaces for its power and water tanks. Use a dry-erase marker to tally 3 units of POWER and 3 units of WATER on the HOUSE tokens at setup.

On each turn, all players use markers to write their chosen squares on their MAIN/CROSS cards (using the coordinates A,B,... and 1,2,...) and place them face-down. These squares are revealed simultaneously. If there is a collision involving two or more players, the exclamation-point token is placed on that square, indicating that the pipes are broken. For all remaining squares, players pick up the tile on that square, flip and rotate them as they choose, and replace them on the same square.

Once all players have played, every house on the board is checked. The POWER/WATER levels on each HOUSE token's tanks are updated using markers, based on whether the houses are connected to POWER/WATER SUPPLY. If either tank reaches zero, the house is removed from the board and discarded.

Playing on a Device

Your chosen square and chosen card are highlighted by a border in your colour. You can try up to five card/square combinations; tap the chosen square again to confirm your move. That square is then marked with grey shading during the next round as a reminder of the move. Trying to play on an off-limits square will trigger a message that explains why this can't be done.

In multi-player mode ("Human" icon) each player's choice is recorded serially, and then all players' moves are replayed together. In Human vs. Bot mode ("Bot" icon) Player 1 is the human, other players are Bots. On your turn you can use the Auto button to autoplay your move. Replay, Bot play, and Autoplay are indicated by a PLAY icon in the score box.
