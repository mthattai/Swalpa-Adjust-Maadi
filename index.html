Swalpa adjust maadi

Bengalurina prema kathe

Competing contractors operate in a Bangalore neighbourhood. Each is trying to supply power and water to as many houses as possible, without much concern for how their actions affect the others. Meanwhile, roads are constantly dug up for Metro work, VIP visits, and floods, wreaking havoc on the infrastructure. The city's residents are left to deal with unpredictable power and water, and must suffer in silence.

Components

  • 1 board
  • 1 setup deck
  • 1 play deck
  • House tokens in each player's colour

The board

A board of 6x6, 7x7, or 8x8 spaces. Two opposite edges supply power, shown in red; the other two supply water, shown in blue. Any space along an edge with a connection reaching it is directly supplied.

The deck

There are 8 card types: 2 Straight, 4 Bend, Roadworks, and Park.

The play deck is fixed, and contains the following per player (so a 2-player game has twice as many of each, and so on):

The deck is shuffled once before play begins, and drawn from without replacement - once a card is drawn, it's gone until the deck is rebuilt for a new game.

The connection rule

When connectors of the same colour meet across an edge separating two cards, the corresponding utility (power or water) can flow across it in either direction. Every red or blue path connected all the way back to its supply is a live connection.

Setup

The board starts with a fixed number of Parks for its size (6, 8, or 10 for a 6x6, 7x7, or 8x8 board), placed so that no single space on the board is adjacent to more than one Park. Every other space is filled with a Straight or Bend card, drawn at random with replacement, using the same relative proportions as the deck above (so a Straight A is twice as likely to appear as a Straight B, for example, matching their 4-to-2 split in the deck). If this setup would produce more than 4 houses immediately (see below), the board is discarded and a new one is drawn, repeating until it comes in at 4 or fewer. Any space already connected to both supplies at this point becomes a house immediately.

Parks

No player may place a card on a Park. Roadworks can still strike a Park like any other space, destroying it. Once that happens, the space is open for play like any other.

Houses and control

Houses are built when an empty space first connects to both red and blue supply. The player on whose turn this happens can henceforth earn points from that house, indicated by the roof of the house being in their colour. You only earn points for houses that are simultaneously connected to both red and blue supply, with houses closer to the centre earning more points. Houses built at setup are not allocated to any player. If Roadworks destroys a Park or house, a new house can be built and claimed on that space.

Stealing: On your turn, place your card on a space. If this space has a house disconnected from both red and blue, and if at least one of the four edgewise neighbours also contains a house of the same colour, you can steal the house. Its roof switches to your colour.

Play

Player 1 goes first, others follow in turn. In 1-player games the human is Player 1 and the computer is Player 2.

The row of 8 icons shows the upcoming cards. All players use the same deck. The rightmost card is the current active card. Once a player plays their card, the entire row moves one step to the right. In the last few rounds, fewer than 8 cards remain in the deck, so this row shrinks; the very last card is used on the final turn of the game.

To play your turn, click a space on the board to place the active card there and see what it does. You can try out any space except for Parks, and the space the previous player just played on (which is greyed out). You have up to five tries, indicated by dots (unlimited in Practice mode). If the active card is Roadworks it lands on a random space, you can't choose where. Roadworks destroys any Park or house it lands on (ouch!), leaving the space up for grabs in subsequent turns. The current chosen space is always highlighted by an orange border. Click the chosen space again to confirm your move and complete your turn.

Once the card is played, the row of upcoming cards moves one step to the right. The current active card is updated and the next player plays their turn.

Scoring

Each round consists of one move by each player in turn. After their turn, a player earns points for every house in their colour that is simultaneously connected to both red and blue supply. The points-per-house depends on location, increasing closer to centre: houses in the outermost zone are worth 1 point each, houses in the next inner zone are worth 2 points each, and so on. A house that's only partly connected does not contribute to the score. Each player's total is checked and updated only once in a round, on their turn.

Winning

After 20 rounds, whoever has the higher score wins. Equal score is a draw.
