Swalpa adjust maadi - Bengalurina prema kathe

Two contractors operate in a Bangalore neighbourhood. Each is trying to supply power and water to as many houses as possible, without much concern for how their actions affect the other. Meanwhile, roads are constantly dug up for Metro work, VIP visits, and floods, wreaking havoc on the infrastructure. The city's residents are left to deal with unpredictable power and water, and must suffer in silence.

Components

1 board
76 cards in the deck
36 House tokens in each player's colour

The board

A board of 6x6 spaces. Two opposite edges supply power, shown in red; the other two supply water, shown in blue. Any space along an edge with a connection reaching it is directly supplied.

The deck

There are 8 card types.

Straight (2 possible orientations): red and blue connectors, perpendicular to each other.

Bend (4 possible orientations): red and blue L-shaped connectors, on opposite corners.

Roadworks (orientation irrelevant): a yellow triangle and no connectors.

Park (orientation irrelevant): a tree and no connectors.

The deck consists of:

5x6 copies of the Straight/Bend cards for setup.

6 Park cards for setup.

6x6 copies of the Straight/Bend cards for play.

4 Roadworks cards for play.

This gives a total of 36 shuffled cards for setup, and 40 shuffled cards as the playable deck.

The connection rule

When connectors of the same colour meet across an edge separating two cards, the corresponding utility (power or water) can flow across it in either direction. Every red or blue path connected all the way back to its supply is a live connection.

Setup

Fill the board with the 36 shuffled setup cards. Check the board once. Any space already connected to both supplies becomes a house immediately.

Parks

Six of the 36 setup spaces are Parks. Neither player may place a card on a Park. Roadworks can still strike a Park like any other space, destroying it. Once that happens, the space is open for play like any other.

Houses and control

Houses are built when an empty space first connects to both red and blue supply. The player on whose turn this happens can henceforth earn revenue from that house, indicated by the roof of the house being in their colour. Houses built at setup are not allocated to either player. At each turn, each player earns revenue from houses in their colour that are simultaneously connected to both red and blue supply (see "How to score"). If Roadworks destroys a Park or house, a new house can be built and claimed on that space.

Play

Play alternates, Player 1 first. In 1-player games the human is Player 1 and the computer is Player 2.

The row of 8 icons shows the upcoming cards. Both players use the same deck. The rightmost card is the current active card. Once a player plays their card, the entire row moves one step to the right. In the last few rounds, fewer than 8 cards remain in the deck, so this row shrinks; the very last card is used on the final turn of the game.

To play your turn, click a space on the board to place the active card there and see what it does. You can try out as many spaces as you like except for Parks, and the space the other player just played on (which is greyed out). If the active card is Roadworks it lands on a random space, you can't choose where. Roadworks destroys any Park or house it lands on (ouch!), leaving the space up for grabs in subsequent turns. The current chosen space is always highlighted by an orange border. Click the chosen space again to confirm your move and complete your turn.

Once the card is played, the row of upcoming cards moves one step to the right. The current active card is updated and the next player plays their turn.

Scoring

Each round consists of one Player 1 move and one Player 2 move. After every turn, each player earns one point for every house in their colour that is simultaneously connected to both red and blue supply. A house that's only partly connected does not contribute to the score. Both players' totals are checked and updated after every turn, so scores can go up twice each round.

Winning

After 20 rounds, whoever has the higher score wins. Equal score is a tie.
