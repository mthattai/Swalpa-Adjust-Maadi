Swalpa adjust maadi - Bengalurina prema kathe

You play as the Red Power Co. or the Blue Water Co., both operating in a Bangalore neighbourhood. Each is trying to supply as many houses as possible, without much concern for how their actions affect the other. Meanwhile, roads are constantly dug up for Metro work, VIP visits, and floods, wreaking havoc on the infrastructure. The city's residents are left to deal with unpredictable power and water, and must suffer in silence.

Components

1 board
76 cards in the deck
36 House tokens
36 red and 36 blue control markers

The board

A board of 6x6 spaces. Two opposite edges belong to Red's power supply, the other two belong to Blue's water supply. Any space along an edge with a connection reaching it is directly supplied.

The deck

There are 7 card types.

Straight (2 possible orientations): red and blue connectors, perpendicular to each other.
Bend (4 possible orientations): red and blue L-shaped connectors, on opposite corners.
Roadworks (orientation irrelevant): a blank card.

The deck consists of:

6x6 copies of the Straight/Bend cards for setup.
6x6 copies of the Straight/Bend cards for play.
4 copies of Roadworks cards for play.

This gives a total of 36 shuffled cards for setup, and 40 shuffled cards as the playable deck.

The connection rule

When connectors of the same colour meet across an edge separating two cards, the corresponding utility (power or water) can flow across it in either direction. Every red or blue path connected all the way back to its supply is a live connection.

Setup

Fill the board with the 36 shuffled setup cards. Check the board once. Any space already connected to both supplies becomes a house immediately.

Houses and control

Houses are built when a space first connects to both red and blue supply. The player on whose turn this happens henceforth controls the space beneath the house (indicated by red or blue shading). A house built at setup stays neutral for the rest of the game. A house is permanent once built, even if it is later disconnected from supply. If a Roadworks card lands on a house it has no effect.

Play

Play alternates, Red first. In 1-player games the player is Red and the computer is Blue.

The row of 8 icons shows the upcoming cards. Both players use the same deck. The rightmost card is the current active card. Once a player plays their card, the entire row moves one step to the right. In the last few rounds, fewer than 8 cards remain in the deck, so this row shrinks; the very last card is used on the final turn of the game.

To play on your turn, click any space on the board to try the current active card there and see what it does. You can try out as many spaces as you like. The Roadworks card lands on a random space, you can't change it (ouch!). If a Roadworks card lands on a house it has no effect. The current chosen space is always highlighted by an orange border. Click the chosen space again to confirm your move and complete your turn.

Once the card is played, the row of upcoming cards moves one step to the right. The current active card is updated and the next player plays their turn.

Scoring

Each round consists of one red player move and one blue player move. Once a player completes their turn, the total number of houses connected to each colour is tallied. The red total is added to the current red score, and the blue total is added to the current blue score. Both players' scores are simultaneously updated after every turn, so scores go up twice each round.

Winning

After 20 rounds, whoever has the higher score wins. Equal score is a tie.

Notes

Building houses early is important, because it gives you control of the underlying space. A house connected to both red and blue is neutral, since it earns revenue for both players. A player can gain by disconnecting a house from the opponent's supply, or reconnecting a house to their own.
